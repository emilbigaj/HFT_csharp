Execution Simulator Overview
The execution simulator processes historical market-by-price and trade data to maintain an order book reflecting the exact historical state of the market. When a user submits simulated orders, these actions alter the order book, causing it to diverge from the historical state and generate an "alternate reality." The simulator then publishes this updated, alternate market state back to the user.

Terminology
The simulator distinguishes two related but distinct concepts. Confusing them is the most common source of misunderstanding.

  Ghost: phantom maker orders held inside a per-level queue. They are real orders the user's passive fill displaced; the simulator retains them as invisible queue entries so that the user must queue behind them and cannot dominate the level without consequence. Ghost is queue-resident.

  Mask: a quantity used to subtract historical depth from what is published or counted, representing liquidity the simulator has already consumed in its alternate reality but which still appears in raw historical data. Mask is not in any queue; it is an accumulator that adjusts depth at publication time and during cross processing.

Ledger Catalog
The simulator maintains one Ghost ledger and two Mask ledgers:

  Per-level Ghost (QueueManager.Ghost): Created when a user's passive order at a price level is hit by a historical trade. The user "steals" a fill that, in real data, was consumed elsewhere in the queue; the bypassed historical makers are retained as invisible phantom liquidity at that level. Decays on MBP activity (orderbook turnover).

  Cross-induced Mask (OrderManager.CrossMask, side-aggregated): Created when real liquidity moves into a price the user is resting at, causing the simulator to cross-fill market-side depth on the user's side. Built only when MaskCrossed is on, the same flag that gates the Taker Mask from the user's own cross fills, so MaskCrossed off means a cross masks nothing. Masks depth on the opposing side during cross processing. Side-aggregated and price-independent by design: the other participants are presumed to be market makers quoting around fair value, and a maker we crossed with holds back before refreshing (typically until it has exited on the other side), so its liquidity stays spent even after the price has moved, which matters most in illiquid markets. Decays with same-side book activity on its own clock (OrderManager.DecayCrossMask): each same-side MBP or MBO delta returns CrossMask * |delta| / side depth (SideByPrice64.Quantity), with the fractional remainder carried, so a thin book refills slowly and a busy one quickly. It used to decay only through DecayGhost, which needs a resting queue holding Ghost; with MaskMade off no Ghost exists, and the mask then lasted until the session closed.

  Taker Mask (_bidMask, _askMask on the instrument): Created when the user actively takes liquidity, or when a cross-fill at the user's quote is generated. Masks depth on the side that was consumed, both at publication time and during subsequent cross processing. Decays only on historical trades (the real market is presumed to be eating the volume the user already took).

Bookkeeping Invariant
At every QueueManager the following invariant is preserved across all operations (trade, MBP delta, decay):

    MarketQuantity_at_level = Real_depth_at_level + Ghost_at_level

Every code path that mutates Ghost also mutates MarketQuantity (or vice versa) to preserve this. AddGhost establishes it on trade events; DecayGhost reduces both Ghost and MarketQuantity together; ReduceMarketBy mirrors real cancellations on MarketQuantity without touching Ghost.

Trade Timing Assumption
The simulator assumes that for any historical trade, the Trade tick arrives before the corresponding MarketByPrice update reflects that trade's effect on depth. Internally, AddGhost reads SideByPrice.GetQuantity at trade time (pre-update) and offsets it against a per-level _traded accumulator; the next MBP delta cancels the trade-induced component via "delta += _traded; _traded = 0". Reordering these inputs in a custom data feed will break Ghost accounting.

The MBO path has the same assumption at a finer grain. The trade removes its quantity from the front of the queue and adds it to _traded, and the Reduce/Cancel records that remove the filled resting orders are then skipped up to _traded. A MarketByOrder tick carries a bid array and an ask array, split by side with stream order kept within each: a Trade rides its AGGRESSOR's array, while the removals of the orders it filled ride the RESTING side's. InstrumentSimulator.OnMarketByOrder therefore runs every Trade in both arrays first and only then the Add/Reduce/Cancel deltas of both arrays. Walking bids-then-asks (fixed 2026-09-25) was correct for a buy aggressor, whose Trade sits in the bid array ahead of the ask-side removals, but for a sell aggressor it applied the bid-side removals before their Trade: the bid queue lost the traded quantity twice, user bids within that distance of the front were filled on volume that went to the orders ahead of them, and _traded then swallowed the next genuine cancels at that level.

MaskMade (Ghost on or off)
ExchangeSimulator.MaskMade, beside MaskCrossed and MaskTaken, switches Ghost creation; it defaults to true. When a historical trade of Q at a level fills U of our resting quantity there, the market fills are Q - U. With MaskMade on, QueueManager.OnTrade adds the whole Q to _traded, so every fill-removal the feed sends for that trade is skipped and the U market lots reality filled stay in our queue as Ghost (AddGhost). With it off, only the Q - U the simulator removed itself go into _traded; the other U fill-removals are applied by PriorityId and take out exactly the orders the data says filled. The queue then tracks the real book, Ghost stays 0, and DecayGhost, ReduceGhost and the queue manager's keep-alive have nothing to act on. Off is the optimistic assumption: our fills cost no historical maker its fill, and a later order at that price does not queue behind the displaced makers. Exact only for MBO data; on the MBP path OnMarketByPriceDelta folds _traded into the level delta, so the U lots come off by the behind-heavy cancel distribution instead of from the front. The taker masks (MaskCrossed, MaskTaken) are unaffected.

PriorityId Baseline (MBO)
A delete of PriorityId p is applied to the first market blob whose id is >= p, and a user order is stamped with OrderManager.PriorityId, the highest id seen, when it joins a queue. That is exact only while every order that joins the queue after the user carries a higher id than the stamp. PriorityId rises by one per Add within a day, but the sequence restarts at every snapshot: the parser renumbers the whole resting book at each daily R snapshot, and the tick-history files stitch conversion runs with different bases (6E Sep26: 1.26e14 on 2026-08-21, 2.64e14 on Sunday 08-23, 1.72e14 on 08-24, 1.18e14 on 08-25, with two snapshots at each midnight). OrderManager.PriorityId used to be a running maximum that never came down, so after Sunday every user order was stamped above every real id and EVERY cancel at the level, including those of orders queued behind the user, was taken off the blob in front of it. Measured against the true arrival-order queue on 08-24, that left 28-30% of orders 3 ticks deep unfilled at zero ahead after 5 minutes, against 0-1% in reality.
At every MarketByOrderSnapshot, OrderManager.OnMarketByOrderSnapshot now restarts the baseline from the snapshot's highest id and re-stamps each live queue with QueueManager.RestampPriorityIds: the level's snapshot orders ascending by id are the queue head to tail, each market blob takes the id of the snapshot order where its cumulative quantity lands, and each user order takes the id of the blobs in front of it. Replayed against the real 6E book (virtual 1-lots at the touch, 3 and 10 ticks deep, horizons 10 s to 1 h, including orders resting across midnight), the simulator's queue then matches the true queue in every case.

Maker Orders
When a user submits a maker order, it is added to the simulator's queue based on price-time priority. New orders queue strictly behind any existing market depth at that level, including invisible Ghost makers.

Cancel Distribution: When historical depth decreases (cancellations occur), the simulator assumes they are distributed cubically across the queue. Each market order is cancelled at a rate proportional to (quantity_ahead_of_it / total_market_quantity)^3, weighting cancellations heavily toward the back of the order queue. Newly placed user orders, sitting near the back of fresh queue formations, are disproportionately likely to be simulated as passed over or canceled if the market thins out. This protects front-of-queue positions and adversarially punishes deeper queue placement.

Ghost Creation: If a user's passive maker order is filled, it effectively steals a fill that would have otherwise gone to a historical maker. In our alternate reality, we assume those bypassed historical orders remain in the queue as invisible Ghost orders. This prevents the trader from dominating the book without consequence. Per-level Ghost is created in three scenarios:
  - A historical trade at the user's level fills user quantity: Ghost increases by the user's fill amount.
  - A historical trade at a less aggressive level consumes phantom market still tracked at this level: Ghost decreases (the simulator is catching up to real).
  - A cross-fill consumes market-side depth at this level: Ghost decreases here and CrossMask increases on the side.

Initial Depth at Empty Levels: When a user places a passive order at a price less aggressive than any currently known level (for example, bidding below the lowest visible bid), the new QueueManager is seeded with the depth observed at the worst-known level. This is a conservative assumption: the simulator presumes at least as much queue volume sits ahead at that deeper level as exists at the edge of the visible book.

Ghost Decay: Ghost orders are dynamically reduced over time, decaying proportionally to ongoing order book activity. For each MBP delta of magnitude D occurring anywhere in the book, every Ghost-bearing level decays by approximately Ghost * D / TotalMarketQuantity_snapshot, where the snapshot is a Ghost-weighted blend of the book's total depth captured at the moments Ghost was added. This normalization means a fixed-size cancel decays Ghost faster in a thin book than in a deep one. The simulator assumes the alternate reality naturally heals back to historical reality over time as actual market volume absorbs the localized impact.

Execution Logic: If the historical best bid crosses the user's maker sell order (or vice versa), the user's order is immediately filled. When the historical book is itself in a crossed or locked state (best bid >= best ask in raw data), the matching engine is presumed not to be running continuously (an auction phase). For the duration of this state no passive user fills are produced and no Ghost or Mask is accumulated; cross processing resumes once the book uncrosses.

More-Aggressive Walk on Historical Trades: When a historical trade arrives at price T, the simulator first consumes any tracked liquidity at prices more aggressive than T. These represent user orders and Ghost market that real data has already cleared in flight but the simulator was still carrying. Trade quantity is allocated through those levels in price-priority order before reaching the reported level T; only the residual quantity reaches the trade-level QueueManager.

Crossed Market Sweeps: During a crossed book event (a real aggressive price crossing into our user's quote without the raw book being in auction), the matching engine first deducts any existing Mask quantities from the historical aggressor level (Taker Mask on this side plus CrossMask from the opposing manager) before executing the user's order against the remaining valid volume.

Order Amendments: Modifying an existing maker order to decrease its quantity safely preserves its exact position in the queue. However, increasing the quantity or altering the price forces a full cancellation and re-insertion, instantly dropping the order to the absolute back of the queue.

Restoration of Depth: When a user's maker order is canceled or completed, the simulator flags the specific price level. On the very next market data update, the underlying historical depth is overwritten back into the published book at that level before user quantities are re-applied. This ordering matters when a user immediately re-enters at the same level: the flag-and-restore step runs first, then the new user quantity is layered on top, ensuring the alternate reality dynamically mends itself without double-counting.

Queue Visibility: The simulator actively calculates and publishes exact queue position updates (via an "Ahead of Order" message) back to the client. The reported quantity-ahead includes both real market depth and Ghost market still in the queue, so the user's queue estimate is conservative by construction.

User Visibility and Impact: To maintain a conservative, adversarial simulation, traders cannot see or trade against extra Ghost orders. If a trader submits a new maker order, it must queue behind any existing invisible Ghost makers at that price level. The core purpose of Ghost orders is to realistically increase queue wait times, heavily penalizing the trader for liquidity they previously absorbed.

Taker Orders
Users can actively cross the spread to take liquidity from the historical order book.

Liquidity Consumption: The simulator ensures a user cannot double-dip historical liquidity. If a trader aggressively sweeps a level, the spread visibly widens, punishing overly aggressive sizing.

Walking the Book: If a taker order's quantity exceeds the available liquidity at the best historical price level, the simulator natively walks the order book. It sweeps consecutive levels, accumulating distinct fills (and generating corresponding Taker Mask) until the requested quantity is entirely satisfied.

Masked Liquidity: Every time a user takes historical liquidity, the system records the consumed volume into the per-side Taker Mask (_bidMask or _askMask). This quantity is removed (masked) from the historical state in all subsequent published depth, strictly enforcing liquidity conservation.

Top-Down Peeling: The simulator processes masked taker liquidity strictly from the top of the book downwards. When masking consumed liquidity from the user, it peels volume starting directly from the best bid or ask, accurately simulating how aggressive sweeping degrades the top-of-book pricing.

Mask Decay: Taker Mask is reduced dynamically whenever actual historical trades occur (subtracting the trade quantity from the side's Taker Mask balance). The simulator assumes the historical trade essentially "ate" the volume the user already took, allowing the book to gradually resynchronize with pristine historical data. Note this decay clock differs from Ghost decay, which runs on MBP activity rather than trades.

Immediate Latency Impact: Taker fills are generated immediately upon crossing the historical spread, but the corresponding fill notifications are subjected to strict Exchange-to-NIC microsecond latency delays before reaching the strategy.

User Visibility and Impact: The user explicitly sees the impact of their aggressive actions; any historical quantities they have taken are visibly removed from the order book published to them. This prevents strategies from backtesting against the assumption of infinite liquidity at the historical top-of-book.

Order Entry
InstrumentSimulator.Target applies each OrderTarget the server lets through, against the simulator's own copy of the order state. The rules below were added on 2026-10-04.

Time in Force: The order state the simulator creates for a Create copies TimeInForce from the OrderTarget. Before, it was left at Day (0), so an IOC rested in the simulated book like a Day order. The Algo sends only Day (0) and ImmediateOrCancel (2): Algo.Target/TryTarget throw NotImplementedException for any other value before sending. Nothing else validates TimeInForce, though: neither ValidateOrder (client or server) nor Server.OnOrderTarget checks it. A target sent straight through Client.OnOrderTarget with any other value passes the server and is modelled as Day here, so a FillOrKill would partially fill and rest. The published OrderState carries TimeInForce, but Server.WriteOrderState does not copy it. The server row's value comes from its own PendingNew seed in Server.OnOrderTarget.

Marketable and IOC Orders: In Enqueue, an order that is marketable (IsMarketable: the book is not crossed, the opposite side has a level, and the limit is at or through the opposite best; masks are not considered, so Take may still fill nothing) or is an IOC has queue position 0 ahead and 0 behind. That position is published through OnQueuePosition, never written straight into the copy. OnQueuePosition sends AheadOfOrder only when the pair differs from the copy, and after its PendingNew seed the server row changes only on AheadOfOrder. So a direct write of 0 (as before 2026-10-04) made the copy agree with a value the server never received. The server row then stayed at its PendingNew seed (the book quantity at the price), and the next real 0/0 publish was suppressed as unchanged. A new order's copy starts at QuantityAhead = -1 (2026-09-26), so the 0/0 of a Create always goes out. The ack (OrderState, reason Acked) follows. Acceptance-before-trade holds for IOCs too: the ack always precedes the fills.
  - An IOC never rests. If it is marketable it takes (Take) what it can within its limit. Any remainder is then Eliminated at once: Update with OrderStateReason.Eliminated (6), which is >= Canceled and so Done. The terminal state keeps the order's own profile (FIX shape, as for a cancel). A non-marketable IOC is acked and then Eliminated whole with QuantityFilled 0. So is one sent while the simulated book is crossed (IsMarketable is false) and one whose levels within the limit are entirely masked (Take produces no fill).
  - Eliminated has no special case outside the simulator. It travels alone as a non-Fill OrderState, and the server treats it as any Done: WriteOrderState, then RiskLayer's Done branch releases worst - |QuantityFilled|, then the client frees the slot. The IOC's reservation is therefore held from send until this state (the ActiveTargets enumerator never yields an IOC). Realtime: the iLink adapter must deliver CME's IOC elimination as an OrderState with OrderStateReason.Eliminated, status Done and the cumulative QuantityFilled.
  - A marketable Day order takes, then rests whatever survives the sweep at its limit. It gets no second ack. EnqueueUserOrder re-publishes the remainder's position, which is normally still 0/0: nothing in the market rests on its own side at a price it could sweep (the new level is seeded from GetMarketQuantity, 0 there), so OnQueuePosition sends nothing unless one of our own orders is already queued at that price.
  - A reprice (an amend that loses priority) goes through Enqueue too, so an amended IOC would be eliminated the same way. The Algo never amends IOCs: they are never active targets.
Example: best ask 100 x 3, 101 x 5, nothing masked. An IOC buy 10 @ 101 produces, on the instrument's execution channel: AheadOfOrder(0, 0); OrderState(Acked); OrderState(Fill)+Fill 3 @ 100; OrderState(Fill)+Fill 5 @ 101; OrderState(Eliminated, Done, QuantityFilled 8, profile still 10 @ 101). The same order as Day stops after the second fill and rests 2 @ 101 on the bid side, with no further ack and no further AheadOfOrder: that bid level is empty, so its position (0 ahead, 0 behind) equals the 0/0 already sent.

Refused Targets Change Nothing: For a target on a live order, every reason that can refuse a cancel is evaluated before the cancel path: SeqOutOfOrder (stored Seq >= target Seq), NotInSession (TradingStatus not Open), StrategyIdNotValid and InstrumentIdNotValid. If any is set, Target returns the state untouched, and ExchangeSimulator.OnOrderTarget sends an OrderRejected with source Exchange. Before 2026-10-04 two things went wrong here. The stored Seq was overwritten right after the SeqOutOfOrder test, so a stale target rewound it. And the cancel path (Delete) ran even with reasons set, so a cancel refused for SeqOutOfOrder or NotInSession both deleted the order and was rejected. An exchange rejects or applies, never both. The stored Seq is now overwritten only once those four checks pass. Two refusals still come after that point: TargetIsActive (target profile equals the state profile) and SideNotValid (side change). An amend refused for one of them leaves the order unchanged, but its stored Seq has already advanced to the refused target's Seq.
A refused Create is the exception on the server side. The simulator's slot is untouched (the not-found branch refuses for NotInSession, ClientOrderIdOutOfOrder, or QuantityNotValid + SideNotValid), but ServerSimulator.OnExchangeOrderRejected first feeds the server a synthetic Done/Rejected OrderState (QuantityFilled 0, QuantityAhead 0) through Server.OnOrderState, and only then the reject, because the real session delivers a rejected new order's terminal state separately. Open (pending CME): confirm iLink always sends that terminal state. If it does not, the synthesis moves into Server.OnOrderRejected, in C# and C++ together.
Example: Buy 5 @ 100 resting with stored Seq 3. A cancel with Seq 3 is rejected SeqOutOfOrder and the order stays live with Seq 3 (before 2026-10-04 it was deleted as well). A later cancel with Seq 4 is applied.
The found-order NotInSession refusal is not reachable in the simulator. It produces Open and Closed only, and OnTradingStatus(Closed) cancels every live order (CancelAllOrders) and clears the queues first, so no order rests while the session is closed. A target that arrives after the close finds no live order and is refused on the not-found path (NotInSession + OrderNotFound). The "order stays live" case would apply only to a non-Open status that does not cancel orders, such as a venue halt, which the simulator does not model.

Amend Classification (Reduce vs Replace): OrderTargetAction.Amend was renamed Replace (wire value 1 unchanged) and Reduce = 3 was added. The simulator, like CME, never reads the action byte to tell the two apart: anything that is not Create or Cancel takes the same amend path, and the outcome is decided from the simulator's own state. An amend whose total quantity is at or below the filled quantity is a cancel of the remainder (unchanged). Otherwise, with quantityDelta = sign * (target quantity - state quantity):
  - A price change, or quantityDelta > 0, is a Reprice: Delete then Enqueue, back of the queue.
  - quantityDelta < 0 at the same price is a Reduce: OrderManager.Reduce, queue position kept.
  - An unchanged profile is refused TargetIsActive.
The sender's label is only its belief about the working order. Example: Buy 10 @ 100 resting. A Replace to 12 @ 100 is refused by the server (say TooManyOrdersPerSecond) and never reaches the simulator. The sender, which had not yet seen the reject, then sends 11 @ 100 labelled Reduce (relative to the 12 it believed working). The simulator compares against its state of 10, so this is a Reprice to the back of the queue, not a Reduce. The server's rate-limit exemption for a Reduce is checked the same way, against the server's state row (OrderProfile.IsReduceOf), not the label alone.

Startup: the Pre-Clock Admin Drain
Clients allocate their instruments before the clock starts. ServerSimulator.Init therefore runs a thread ("{ServerName}.Init()") that calls Server.ReadAdmin in a loop until Clock.IsRunning. That thread is outside every Clock callback. Before 2026-10-04 an exception there was unhandled and killed the process: an ArgumentOutOfRangeException from OnAllocateInstrument, or missing spread legs in Context.CreateInstrument. (A non-calendar spread is now refused client-side in Client.GetInstrument; that check briefly lived in Context.CreateInstrument during development and was never committed.) Each ReadAdmin call is now wrapped in try/catch and reported through Clock.OnException, which raises Clock.Exception. Strategy/Scenario.cs and the Workspace wire that event to AlertManager.OnException (Proxy/Scenario.cs writes it to the console). The loop keeps polling, so a failed allocation is alerted while the others proceed. With no subscriber, the exception is dropped. Once the clock runs, the in-run ReadExecution and the throttled ReadAdmin in OnInterject are already inside the Clock's Interject callback. RunSimulation's loop catches that callback's exceptions and raises them on Clock.Exception. The one final Interject that RunSimulation makes after its loop ends is outside the loop's try/catch. An exception there still reaches Clock.Exception through Clock.Start's own try/catch, but it skips the s_isRunning = false that follows it (Stopped still fires from Start's finally). The contract for realtime callers is in Server.cs: wrap ReadAdmin and ReadExecution in try/catch and call AlertManager.OnException. Open: the single OnInterject(Timestamp.MinValue) the Init thread makes after the loop ends is not wrapped. An exception from its ReadExecution is unhandled on that thread and ends the process, the same failure as before this change. simReady is then never set, so the Clock.Started handler would spin. Its ReadAdmin is skipped by the one-second throttle at Timestamp.MinValue.

Exchange-to-Client Channels
The exchange simulator runs in ExchangeTimestamp order, but Exchange-to-NIC latency differs by market segment (ES, NQ, BTC and 6E each sit on their own segment with their own latency), so one FIFO keyed by NicTimestamp would hold an early NIC behind a later one from another segment and release it late. ServerSimulator therefore keeps one FIFO per instrument per channel: market data (book updates, trades, settlements, TradingStatus) and execution reports (OrderState, Fill, OrderRejected, AheadOfOrder), mirroring MDP and iLink, which are separate paths at CME (execution reports routinely beat the market data). AheadOfOrder rides the execution channel with the acks because OnQueuePosition sends only changes against the copy the ack carried, so the two must not reorder. Recorded NicTimestamps are monotonic within one instrument, so each queue is ordered by construction. OnInterject offers every queue head to Clock, which keeps the minimum; the clock therefore lands exactly on the earliest head, and draining each marked instrument up to now in OnTickTock is an exact merge (ties across instruments carry no order). An entry the simulator stamps itself (a book republish after a user order, stamped Clock.Now + FromNicToClientLatency) can be earlier than the recorded entry ahead of it; it waits for it, as a FIFO channel would, but keeps its own earlier NicTimestamp.

Ghost Lifecycle Summary

  Event                                                          Effect on per-level Ghost
  -------------------------------------------------------------  -----------------------------------------------------
  Historical trade at user's level fills user quantity           += user_fill (via AddGhost)
  Historical trade at less aggressive level consumes phantom     -= phantom_consumed (via ReduceGhost)
  Cross-fill at user's level consumes market-side depth          -= consumed; opposing-side CrossMask += consumed
  Any MBP delta anywhere in the book                             -= Ghost * |delta| / TotalMarketQuantity_snapshot
  User maker order canceled or fully filled with Ghost > 0       Level marked for removal; QM retained until Ghost decays to zero

Mask Lifecycle Summary

  Event                                                          Effect on Mask
  -------------------------------------------------------------  -----------------------------------------------------
  User actively takes liquidity                                  Taker Mask on consumed side += fill_quantity
  Cross-fill against user maker                                  Taker Mask on aggressor side += user_fill
  Cross-fill consumes market-side depth at user's level          CrossMask on user's side += consumed
  Historical trade on a side                                     Taker Mask on that side -= min(trade_quantity, mask)
  Same-side MBP/MBO delta with CrossMask > 0                     CrossMask -= CrossMask * |delta| / side depth
